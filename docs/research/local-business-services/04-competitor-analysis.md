## 1. Method (planning § 5.3)

Set A 8–10, Set B 3–5 per new service, Set C 6–8 by rank. Three pages per competitor (home, pricing or
nearest, one service page), retrieval dated; one representative URL in the table. Record positioning
(≤ 15-word quote), pricing transparency ∈ {Published, From-price, Hidden, Blocked}, £ price + billing

(`PECR, ICO, GDPR, DMCC, TPS, Ofcom, consent, opt-in, opt-out`), proof claims counted, UK-ness,
affiliate disclosure. Write-up wording is bounded: "We checked N … on <date>. On the pages we could
reach, M of N mentioned <token>." Never disparage; disclose any affiliate status.

### N.3 What they do well — steal this

- **Published two-part prices** (XCONS, Softomate): set-up plus monthly, on the page, with "no
  minimum contract" stated — the display shape § 4.7 adds.
- **The low-entry ladder** (SMB Booster £25 / £49 / £97; Hand On Web receptionist from £149): a
  receptionist plan under £100 a month exists in the market; 07's ladders must sit knowingly above or
  beside it with the reason stated (disclosures, fallback, minutes at cost, human replies).
- **PECR literacy on a GoHighLevel page** (Softomate ×12, Automate to Grow with the soft opt-in
  sentence): the argument WebAsk's CRM page makes is already in the SERP twice — the pages must go
  further (the content test for service messages, the DMCC review layer, regs 19/21 for AI calls, the
  hosted-data transfer chain), not restate PECR.
- **Company number and ICO registration in the footer** (Softomate, Hand On Web): R46 for clients,
  and WebAsk's own company-information page already does the equivalent.
- **Bounded named proof** (We Are Marketable's five testimonials with person and company; Automate to
  Grow's four logos): the form WebAsk can only earn, never simulate.

### Q.6 What Set B changes in the plan

- **Text-back ladder (07):** the self-serve band is £6.49–£97 per month, with conversation
  allowances the sensible unit (Call2SMS 36/135/225). WebAsk's plan sits above it only on the
  done-for-you and compliance design: a service-message template (two of four samples on the market
  carry a "book today" line or an emoji that doubles the segment), the number registered to the client,
  and no enrolment — say so on the page (M.1 § 2, § 4). The 100-conversation allowance in I.1 is in the
  market's range.
- **AI receptionist ladder (07):** unlimited minutes at £49 (HeyJodie) and £10 + £0.85 per call (VoIP
  Shop) exist; a £199 plan needs its reasons printed (disclosures in the first sentence, fallback to a
  person, tuning, the report, minutes at cost) or a lower entry tier — Q8 for the brainstorm. **None
  of the three UK providers states that the AI tells callers it is an AI** — M.3 § 2 is the gap.
- **VAT display precedents (07 § 5):** BookedSolid prints "ex VAT"; HeyJodie prints "Prices exclude
  applicable taxes… added at checkout"; every other Set B and Set A page is silent. "+ VAT where
  applicable" stays until D2, and the comparables table records who states what.
- **A UK per-SMS retail rate exists:** Phorest's 7p–9.5p per message is a published GBP figure to cite
  in 07 § 3 when explaining why usage is passed through on a schedule rather than hidden in a plan.
- **Reviews (07, 04):** UK-native platforms (Trustpilot) sell invitations on 12-month prepaid USD
  contracts; the US tools quote in USD or hide prices. No Set B review page mentions the DMCC Act,

## The campaigns your CRM build stopped at

A CRM build ends on the day the system works: the forms capture, the pipeline moves, the sequences are wired and the person who owns it has been trained. What it does not include — deliberately, and it says so — is the writing and sending that follows, week after week, for as long as the business runs. That is not a gap in the build. It is a different kind of work, with no end date, and it belongs on a monthly plan.

This is that plan. Two campaigns a month, one automation built or reviewed, a list kept clean, and a report tied to replies and bookings. Run from your platform, in your account or on a plan we host, to the people you are allowed to message — which in the UK is the part that takes a month to establish before anything is sent.

## "Who can we legally email or text in the UK?"

Not everyone on your list, and the answer turns on what kind of person the address belongs to rather than what it looks like. A limited company, an LLP or a public body is a corporate subscriber, and PECR's consent rule does not apply to it. A sole trader, an unincorporated partnership, or anyone on a personal address or number is an individual subscriber, and the rule does: nobody may send them unsolicited marketing by email or text "unless the recipient of the electronic mail has previously notified the sender that he consents". A text counts as electronic mail, so the same rule covers SMS.

There is one exception worth quoting exactly, because it is narrower than the version people carry in their heads. The soft opt-in applies where the sender "has obtained the contact details of the recipient … in the course of the sale or negotiations for the sale of a product or service to that recipient", the marketing is "in respect of that person's similar products and services only", and the recipient was given "a simple means of refusing … at the time that the details were initially collected, and … at the time of each subsequent communication". A bought list never qualifies. A list of people who enquired three years ago and were never told they could refuse does not qualify either.

The full argument, with the table, is on the [CRM page](/services/crm-automation). This page applies it: before a campaign is written, every record on your list is tagged by legal form and by the basis on which it may be messaged, and the campaign goes only to the records that carry one.

## Month one is the consent audit

The first month of the plan sends nothing. It reads the list. For each record: where did it come from, what was the person told, did they refuse, and is the address personal or corporate. Records with a basis are tagged and segmented. Records without one are not deleted — they are parked. They are not emailed or texted to ask for permission either: the ICO counts contacting people to ask for consent to direct marketing as direct marketing itself, so a request for consent sent by the channel you have no consent for is the very message you may not send. An individual with no basis can be asked by post, on a live call that has cleared the Telephone Preference Service screen, or at their next visit — and a limited company can simply be emailed.

The number of records that survive that audit is the first figure about your list you can stand behind, and wherever consent was never recorded it will be smaller than the contact count. That is not a loss. A list you can defend is worth more than a larger one you cannot, and it is the only list an automation should ever be pointed at, because an automation runs at volume, unattended, every day.

## Two campaigns a month, one automation — what that means month to month

A campaign is a message with a purpose: a seasonal reminder, a new service, a price change, a piece of advice worth sending. Two a month is enough to be remembered and few enough to be read. Each is written to your brief, in your voice, checked against the rules that apply to your sector, sent from your platform to the segment that may receive it, and reported on two weeks later.

An automation is a sequence that runs on a trigger: a welcome after an enquiry, a follow-up after a quote, a rebooking prompt at the interval your service runs on, a win-back when a customer has gone quiet. One is built or reviewed each month, and every one carries the lawful-basis filter in front of it — the sequence checks who it may message before it sends, not after.

Every message names who sent it and carries a way to stop, because regulation 23 requires both and because a message without them is the kind that gets reported.

## "Database reactivation" done lawfully

The phrase comes from the platform world, where it is used to mean sending an offer to everyone who ever gave you a number. Done that way it is a marketing text to a list with no consent behind it, and an automation makes the mistake at scale.

Done lawfully it is smaller and better: the records that carry a basis get a message written to bring them back, and the records that do not are left alone by email and text, because a message asking for permission is itself a marketing message. If those people are worth reaching, the routes are a letter, a live call after a TPS screen, or the next time they walk in. In the first quarter of the plan that is the reactivation campaign — on the records that can carry it, and only those.

## SMS and WhatsApp: what they cost and who may receive them

Texts and WhatsApp messages are metered by the platform and billed in US dollars; we charge them at cost, at the pound rates on our published schedule, which is reset each quarter against the exchange rate. The plan fee itself is fixed in pounds for twelve months, so the only line that moves with the exchange rate is the one that moves with what you send.

Who may receive them is the same PECR question as email, with one addition for WhatsApp: WhatsApp's own business policy requires that the customer has given you the number and opted in to messages on WhatsApp before anything is sent there. That makes WhatsApp a channel for people who asked for it, never a channel for first contact.

## Clinics and dental practices: what a message may say

For a clinic, an automated email or text that promotes a treatment is an advert, because the advertising code applies to organic content and not only to paid ads. A prescription-only medicine may not be advertised to the public, and the regulator treats the euphemisms as promotion of the medicine; the compliant message promotes the consultation. The [aesthetic clinics](/industries/aesthetic-clinics) page sets out the rule, and every campaign for a clinic is written to it before it is written to convert.

For a dental practice the recall reminder and the promotional message are kept as two flows, each with its own consent state: this plan's campaigns are the second only; the recall is built or reviewed as an automation, stays a service message and sits outside the campaign calendar — the [dental practices](/industries/dental-practices) page has the rest.

## Solicitors: existing clients only

The Solicitors Regulation Authority's code says a solicitor must not make "unsolicited approaches to members of the public, with the exception of current or former clients, in order to advertise legal services". For a law firm, that settles who this plan may message: current and former clients, and nobody else, whatever the list says. A campaign that would reach the public is one we will not send for a firm, and we will say so before the brief.

## The monthly report: replies and bookings, not open rates

Open rates are a number your email software invents from tracking pixels that many inboxes now block or pre-fetch, and they tell you nothing about whether anyone did anything. The report counts what you can check: replies received, bookings or enquiries that followed a campaign, opt-outs, and the size of the list that may still be messaged. If a campaign produced no replies, that is in the report, with a view on why.

## Two limits, stated up front

We do not buy, rent or supply lists, and we will not import one you were sold. A bought or scraped address can never carry soft opt-in, whatever the seller said, and the domain that pays for sending to it is yours.

Nor does this plan build the CRM. If the system underneath is not yet capturing consent at entry, the build comes first and is priced as a project on the [CRM page](/services/crm-automation); the plan starts once there is a list worth sending to.

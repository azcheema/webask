## A profile we do not have, and why that matters to yours

WebAsk has no Google Business Profile and will not create one. We work remotely with no UK office, and Google's rules require either a premises customers can visit, staffed in the hours you state, or a business that travels to its customers; a rented mailing address, a mailbox or an unstaffed desk in a coworking space is neither, and Google says it may suspend a profile that breaks its guidelines. So we are ineligible, and we would rather say that on the page than pretend otherwise.

It is also why we are the right people to run yours. The profile is free, and it is one of the few things a local business can fix in an afternoon that changes what a searcher sees — and the same rules that shut us out are the ones that decide whether yours stays up. We know them because we had to read them to find out we could not have one.

## Are you eligible?

Google's own test is short: "If your business either has a physical location that customers can visit, or travels to customers where they are, you can create a Business Profile on Google." A shop, a salon, a practice or a garage qualifies on the first half. A plumber, a mobile dog groomer or a cleaner qualifies on the second, as a service-area business — and Google says such businesses "should hide your business address from customers", showing the area served instead.

What does not qualify: an address you rent but do not work from ("a virtual office … isn't eligible"), and a coworking office unless it has signage, receives customers and is staffed by your people in business hours. If your only address is one of those, the honest advice is to fix the premises question before the profile, and we will tell you so on the first call rather than after a suspension.

## Your account, our management — never the other way round

The profile belongs to your business and lives in your Google account. We are added as a manager — Google's guidelines require a third party to have the owner's notice and consent, a requirement we read as applying to everything we do on the profile, so nothing there is changed without your say-so — and you can remove us in a minute without losing anything. We never create a profile in our name for a client, never hold the owner role, and never verify a location we have not confirmed you operate from.

## What "run properly" means

Many profiles are claimed in an afternoon and never opened again. Running one properly is a list, and we work through it in order: the primary and secondary categories chosen against what people actually search; every service you offer listed with a description; attributes, opening hours, holiday hours and the phone number that is answered; photographs that show the premises, the people and the work; a description written for a customer rather than a search engine; and a name, address and phone that match your website character for character. For a vet practice that is part of a group, the CMA's Order requires the group's name to appear in map results from 22 March 2027 — a rule that exists only for that sector, and one your profile has to meet.

## "Google Business Profile suspended" — reinstatement without the myths

A suspension is Google deciding your profile breaks a rule, and the reinstatement process is Google's, on Google's timetable. We will read the profile against the guidelines, fix what breaks them, gather the evidence Google asks for and file the appeal properly — and we will not promise a date or an outcome, because neither is ours to give. What we can say is which rules the profile breaks, and that is the part worth having in writing.

## Posts, questions and photos every week

A profile that changes is a profile that stays accurate, and accuracy is the part in your control. Each week: a post about something real — a job, an offer that is lawful to advertise, a change of hours — new photographs guided by a short brief to you, and every question in the Questions and Answers section answered before a stranger answers it for you. Suspicious competitor listings — the keyword-stuffed name, the address nobody works from — are reported through Google's process, and what Google does with them is Google's decision.

## Listings beyond Google

Bing Places feeds Copilot and ChatGPT's search. Apple Business Connect feeds Maps on every iPhone. Yell, FreeIndex and the directories your customers still use carry your details whether you maintain them or not, and a wrong phone number on any of them costs you the same as a wrong one on Google. We keep the ones that matter consistent by hand, and where a sync tool helps, it is added at cost and shown as its own line.

## Your website has to say the same things

Google reads your site to confirm the profile, and so does the law. A limited company's website must carry its registered name, number, place of registration and registered office; anyone trading online must show their name, geographic address, email and, where registered, VAT number, with prices stated clearly including whether they include VAT. If your site is missing them, we list what is missing — we do not tell you a site is compliant, because that is not a verdict we sell — and the fix is a footer and a page.

## What the monthly report shows

Google's own insights: how people found the profile, what they searched, how many called, asked for directions or clicked through, and which photographs and posts were seen. Counts from Google, not from us, in a report you can read in five minutes. Rankings are not in it, because a map-pack position is decided by Google per searcher and per location and no report of ours can promise one.

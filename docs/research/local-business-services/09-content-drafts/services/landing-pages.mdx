## One page, one job

A landing page has one job and no navigation to distract from it: a campaign to land, an offer to take up, a booking to make, a guide to download. It is not a small website. A website has to serve everyone who arrives from anywhere; a landing page serves the people you sent, from one advert, one email or one QR code, and asks them to do one thing.

That is why it is priced as a page. The [web development](/services/web-development) page is honest that a full site starts at £3,500 and that a competent UK freelancer builds a good four- or five-page site for less; neither is the right purchase when the job is a single page for a single campaign, and this page exists so that you are not sold one.

## Builder or code — the trade-off, stated

There are two ways we build a page, and they are not the same.

**In the platform's page builder**, inside your account or your hosted plan. Fast to build, easy for you to duplicate for the next campaign, and wired to the CRM without anything to integrate because it is the CRM. The trade-off is performance: a builder page carries the builder's scripts and styles, and it is not measured against the mobile speed floor our own coded builds are. For a page whose traffic comes from an advert or an email, that can be an acceptable trade. We say so rather than pretend the two are equal.

**In code**, alongside a site we built for you. The same component system, the same speed, the same accessibility, deployed on your own hosting. More expensive per page, slower to turn around, and the right answer when the page has to carry organic search traffic or sit inside a site whose standards you paid for.

The first call settles which, and the reason is written into the scope so that nobody has to remember it later.

## "Landing page design cost" — what moves the price

Three things. Whether the page is built in the builder or in code. Whether the copy is edited from your draft or written for you. And whether it is one page or a short funnel — a landing page, a booking or offer step and a thank-you page, wired and measured as a set. What does not move it is your industry, and what never appears is a promise about how many people will convert, because that depends on the traffic you send and the offer you make, neither of which is ours.

## Wired to your CRM: form, calendar, consent at entry

A page that collects a name and a number is collecting personal data, and the moment it does, two things have to be true. The form captures consent — for what you will send, in words the person can understand, with a box they tick rather than one ticked for them — and records it against the contact, so that every message that follows can check it. And the follow-up message that goes out when they submit says who sent it and how to stop.

Wired that way, the page feeds the pipeline directly: a booking lands in the calendar, an enquiry starts the sequence, and nothing is retyped. Wired any other way, the page is a spreadsheet with a design.

## Cookies on a builder page

A campaign page is built to measure itself, and measurement sets cookies. The rules did not go away when the page got smaller. Consent for anything non-essential is to the UK GDPR standard — freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous — which means accept and reject with equal prominence, nothing pre-ticked, and nothing non-essential set before the visitor chooses.

Since 5 February 2026 there are new exceptions. Storage whose sole purpose is statistical — counting visits to improve the page — can run without consent, provided the visitor is told plainly what it is for and given a simple way to object; so can remembering a preference. Advertising and cross-site tracking are not in the exceptions. Applied to a campaign page: the page's own visit count can be consent-free with an opt-out, and the advert platform's conversion pixel still waits for a yes. We can build the banner to that split, and we read the builder's default settings before launch rather than trusting them.

## Offers and prices on the page

If the page shows a price, it has to say clearly whether it includes VAT, and the charges the customer cannot avoid belong in the figure they see first rather than in a fee added later; the page is built that way. If the page takes a deposit or a booking from a consumer who is not standing in front of you, that is a distance contract under the Consumer Contracts Regulations, and the rules apply: the information the customer must be given before they are bound and, subject to the Regulations' exceptions, a fourteen-day right to cancel. We put the cancellation wording and the terms link on the page, not behind it.

For a clinic, the page is an advert, and the advertising code applies to it in full: the compliant page promotes the consultation, and the [aesthetic clinics](/industries/aesthetic-clinics) page explains what that rules out.

## Measurement: events, tags, the thirty-day report

Every page ships with its events named — form submitted, booking made, button clicked — and its campaign tags handled so that the traffic you paid for is the traffic you can see. Thirty days after launch you get a report: visits, submissions, bookings, the sources they came from, and what we would change. Counts, not benchmarks; there is no industry average in it, because the honest ones are not comparable to your page and the dishonest ones are not worth printing.

## Copy: yours edited, or written for you

The base price assumes you send a draft and we edit it into a page: shorter, in order, with one ask. Writing from a brief is an add-on, scoped on its own. Either way the copy is checked against the rules that apply to your sector before it is checked for persuasion, because a page that converts and then has to come down has converted nothing.

## Who owns the page

You do, with the hosted plan as the labelled exception: a builder page in your own account is yours outright and stays there when the campaign ends; one built inside a hosted plan sits in the sub-account we run and moves with it under the plan's exit terms; a coded page lives in the repository and on the hosting you already own. The domain, the form data, the tags and the analytics are all yours from the first day. If you never work with us again, the page keeps working.

## When it is a website you need

When the page has to rank for search terms rather than receive traffic you send. When one page has become four and each is a different job. When the offer needs a services page, an about page and a way to be found by people who did not click an advert. That is a website, priced as one, on the [web development](/services/web-development) page — and if it is what you need, this page will send you there.

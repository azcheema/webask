A garage's phone problem is everyone's, with a twist: the person who should answer it has their hands inside a car, and the person calling has a car that will not start. The paperwork problem is more particular — the estimate that became the bill, the MOT reminder that arrived after the test was due, the price on the website that did not say whether it included VAT.

All three are fixable with a phone system and a website, and the rules that govern them are short. Here they are.

## The car is on the ramp; the phone is ringing

The missed-call text-back sits behind a divert from the number you already publish. When a call rings out, the caller has a text within seconds — sorry we missed you, reply here, or book — from a number tied to your business, and their reply lands in one inbox on the phone in the workshop or the screen at the desk. The text carries no offer, because the ICO's test for whether a message is marketing is about content, and a message that says you missed the call and how to reach you is a service message until it promotes something. The caller's number is used to reply to that call and for nothing else unless they ask.

When nobody can pick up at all — evenings, the Saturday rush — the same divert can go to an automated assistant that says what it is in its first sentence, books the car in against your diary, takes a message, or puts the caller through. It never calls anyone; outbound is a different thing in law, and a different service.

## MOT and service reminders that stay service messages

Any driver can sign up on GOV.UK for "free MOT reminders by text message or email", sent "one month before your car, van or motorcycle MOT is due". A garage's reminder therefore competes with a free government one, and it earns its place with the two things the government's does not carry: your booking link and your price.

It stays a service message while it says when the test or the service is due and how to book. The ICO lists reminders about appointments among the messages that are not direct marketing, and draws the line at content: "if your service message has elements that are direct marketing, even if that is not the main purpose of your message, then it will count as direct marketing". Add "10% off if you book this week" and you have sent a marketing text to a number you may hold no consent for. So the reminder is tied to the vehicle's due date rather than a marketing calendar, goes only to your own customers, from the number your texts always come from, names the garage and carries a way to stop.

## Prices on the site, the way the Code says

The Motor Ombudsman's Motor Industry Code of Practice for Service and Repair binds only the garages accredited to it, but it is a published statement of what a garage customer can expect, and the Chartered Trading Standards Institute has approved it. "Any price quoted will be inclusive of VAT where applicable, and cover any additional charges such as environmental disposal costs." An Estimate is "a general guide to the cost of the Work required (which could go up or down)", given in writing and labelled as an Estimate; a Quotation is "the firm agreed price to complete the Work requested", labelled as a Quotation; and "the final Invoice should match the Quotation unless the Accredited Business has informed you that further Work or time has been required and you have authorised these additional costs". No deposits "unless the parts required for the Work are bespoke", and "High-pressure selling techniques will not be used."

Beneath the Code sits the law that binds every garage. Any price on a website must be indicated "clearly and unambiguously", including "whether they are inclusive of tax". Under the Consumer Rights Act 2015, "anything that is said or written to the consumer, by or on behalf of the trader, about the trader or the service" becomes a term of the contract if the customer takes it into account, the work is done "with reasonable care and skill", and where no price was fixed the customer pays "a reasonable price for the service, and no more".

So the site shows fixed prices as fixed, labels everything else an estimate, puts the VAT position beside every figure, and states the MOT test fee plainly — with the Code and CTSI logos only where the garage is accredited on the day the page goes live.

## The testing guide's rules stop at the forecourt

The MOT testing guide is specific about the premises: the three-triangles sign "must be displayed in a prominent position, no part higher than 4.5m from the ground and no more than one sign on each road frontage", and "the current MOT test fees and appeals poster (VT9A)" with "the details of how to contact DVSA" go on a notice board. Neither that appendix nor the section on requirements for authorisation, as updated on 22 September 2026, says anything about websites, social media or the logo online.

That cuts both ways. Nothing in the testing guide's premises rules or its authorisation section reaches your website — so there is no rule there for the site to meet, and nothing there to lean on for a claim that is not true. What applies online is the general law above, and the honest position is that a website should say what the forecourt says, and no more than a test can deliver.

## Reviews: not at the desk

The review rules have had a regulator with the power to fine directly since 6 April 2025, and the temptation at a garage is specific: the customer is standing at the counter with the keys in their hand. Google's policy forbids selectively soliciting positive reviews and offering anything for one, and adds a line that matters at that counter: businesses "should not require or pressure users to leave ratings or write reviews while on the premises". The review programme therefore asks every customer once, by text or email, after they have driven away; offers nothing; drafts a reply to each review for your approval; and shows your rating on the site as Google shows it.

## Your Google profile

A garage has premises, so eligibility is straightforward: a "physical location that customers can visit". Managed in your own account with us as a manager, the profile carries the right categories, the services with the test fee where Google allows a price, the hours the phone is answered, photographs of the workshop and the people, and the same name, number and trading details as the site, character for character.

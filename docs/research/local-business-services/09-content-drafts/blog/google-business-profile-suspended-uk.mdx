Before you pay anyone, read what Google itself says the process is. It is shorter than the sales pages, and it is the only version that is not guessing.

## What a suspension is, in Google's words

"We may suspend or disable Business Profiles that don't follow our guidelines." That is the whole explanation Google gives, and it points at the answer: the guidelines are the checklist, and reinstatement is the process of showing that the profile now meets them. A suspension is not a penalty with a fixed term, and it is not a queue you can jump.

## The guideline sections to read first

Google does not publish a league table of causes, so nobody honestly can. What it publishes are the rules, and four sections are the ones to read against your own profile before anything else.

**Eligibility.** "If your business either has a physical location that customers can visit, or travels to customers where they are, you can create a Business Profile on Google." A shop, a clinic or a garage qualifies on the first half; a plumber, a cleaner or a mobile therapist qualifies on the second, as a service-area business.

**The address.** "If your business rents a physical mailing address but doesn't operate out of that location, also known as a virtual office, that location isn't eligible for a Business Profile." A mailbox, a registered-office address you never visit, an accountant's address — none qualifies, and a profile built on one has a problem no appeal can solve. And: "Do not create more than one page for each location of your business, either in a single account or multiple accounts."

**Storefront or service area.** "If you're a service-area business, you should hide your business address from customers." Showing a home address on a profile for a business that works at customers' premises is the guideline ignored, not a detail.

**The name.** "Your name should reflect your business's real-world name, as used consistently on your storefront, website, stationery, and as known to customers." The guidelines rule out taglines, store codes, service or product information and location information in the name field. "Smith Plumbing" is a name; "Smith Plumbing — Emergency Boiler Repair Manchester" is a name with service and location information attached, which the guidelines rule out.

If your profile fails one of those, that is the thing to fix. If it passes all four, read the rest of the guidelines before assuming the suspension is a mistake.

## Fix first, appeal second

Google's first instruction is the one people skip: "Make sure your profile follows all the guidelines." An appeal for a profile that still breaks a rule asks Google to reinstate the thing it suspended. Edit the name, hide the address if you are a service-area business, remove the duplicate, correct the categories, and only then open the appeal.

## The evidence Google asks for

Google's page lists the kinds of document it accepts — business registration, licences, tax certificates, utility bills — and attaches one condition to all of them: "For any documents you submit as evidence, check that the business name and address match the profile you want to make an appeal for." A utility bill in a different trading name, or at a different address from the profile, is evidence against you. Gather the documents that match the profile as it now reads, and if none do, that is a finding about the profile, not about the paperwork.

## The appeal itself

Use Google's own appeals tool, and read two sentences before you start. "Once you open the evidence form, you must submit it within 60 minutes or it won't be attached to your appeal" — so have the files ready before you open it. And "Do not create a new Business Profile for the same business while your appeal is under review" — because a second profile is the duplicate rule broken while you are asking for forgiveness on the first.

## What nobody can promise

Google's page says: "We'll review your appeal and send you an email with a decision." It states no period and no criteria beyond the guidelines. Any provider quoting a reinstatement time or a success rate is quoting something Google does not publish. What a competent helper can do is read the profile against the guidelines, fix what breaks them, assemble the matching evidence and file the appeal properly — and tell you which rules the profile breaks.

## If the address was never eligible

Then the honest path is to fix the premises question, not the appeal. A business that works at customers' locations can hold a profile as a service-area business with the address hidden. A business whose only address is a virtual office cannot hold one at that address, and the choice is between a real premises and no profile. We say this with some feeling: WebAsk works remotely with no UK office, and for exactly that reason we hold no profile of our own and will not create one.

## After reinstatement

A reinstated profile is a profile on notice. Keep the name as it is on the sign, one profile per location, the address hidden if you travel to customers, categories and services that describe what you do, and every detail matching the website character for character. If someone manages it for you, they should be a manager on your account, never the owner, and you should be able to remove them in a minute. The [Google Business Profile management](/services/google-business-profile) page describes that arrangement.

## A checklist before you press "appeal"

1. Read the eligibility, address, service-area and name sections against your profile.
2. Fix every rule the profile breaks before you appeal.
3. Assemble documents whose business name and address match the profile exactly.
4. Have the files ready; the evidence form closes 60 minutes after you open it.
5. Submit one appeal and wait for Google's email.
6. Do not create a new profile for the same business while you wait.
7. If the address was never eligible, fix the premises question rather than filing again.

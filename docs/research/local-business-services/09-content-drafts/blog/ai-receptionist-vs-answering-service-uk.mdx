The call you missed can be answered by a person at another company or by a voice agent on a divert, and the pages that sell each of them sit side by side in the same search results. They are not the same product priced two ways. They differ in who is speaking, what that speaker can do, the hours they cover, and the unit you are billed by. This post sets the two out on the same page, without a price list, so that the choice comes from what your calls are.

## What each one is

A telephone answering service is a receptionist who works for another company and answers in your name. Your line diverts to them, they follow a script you agreed, and they take a message, transfer the call or book into a diary you share. You are buying a person's time, in the hours the service is staffed.

An AI receptionist is a voice agent on the same divert. It answers in your name, books into your calendar, takes a message or answers a question from a set you approved, and hands the call to a person when it cannot. You are buying a scripted agent that works at any hour, with minutes counted rather than people.

## They share the same search results

On 24 September 2026, the google.co.uk results we captured for "ai call answering service uk" listed human answering services and AI products in the same ten, and the questions Google showed beneath them were "Can I get AI to answer my phone calls?" and "How much does a call answering service cost in the UK?" Buyers are comparing the two, which is why this post exists, and why the comparison should be made on what each does rather than on which appears first.

## The unit you pay by

An answering service bills for people's time: a bundle of calls or minutes a month, a rate beyond it, and a premium for evenings, weekends or a dedicated receptionist. The cost rises with the calls you get, and a busy month is an expensive one.

An AI receptionist bills for the agent: a set-up fee for the script, the divert and the wiring, a monthly fee that carries an allowance of minutes, and a published rate for minutes beyond it. The cost rises with talk time, not with call count, and it is the same at three in the morning as at three in the afternoon.

We do not print per-minute figures here, because the platform behind an agent bills in US dollars and any pound figure would be a conversion that moves. The plan's monthly figure, its set-up fee and its allowance are on the receptionist page; the bands UK products charge are on our earlier post about the rules and costs.

## Hours

An agent is on at every hour for the same fee. A person is on when the service is staffed, and out-of-hours cover is either a higher rate or a voicemail. If your missed calls arrive after six and at weekends, that alone decides a lot.

## Who is speaking, and whether they say so

A person does not have to announce that they are a person. An agent should announce that it is not one, and in its first sentence: it names your business, says it is an automated assistant, and offers to book, take a message or put the caller through to someone. We checked three UK AI-receptionist products with published prices on 24 September 2026, and none said on its page that the assistant tells callers it is an AI. That sentence is not a legal formality we could find in statute; it is there because a caller who believes they are speaking to a person, and is not, has been misled about who they are dealing with.

Recording is a separate disclosure and applies to both models. If calls are recorded, callers are told before anything else, whichever model answers; on our plan, recording is off unless you switch it on, and the opening line changes when you do.

## The hand-over

A person transfers a call, or takes a message and passes it on. An agent books, takes a message or hands over to a person, and the honest question is what happens when it cannot: when the caller is upset, when the question is outside the approved set, when the line is poor. The answer should be a fallback you chose, to voicemail or to a named person, and it should be tested from a real phone before the plan goes live. A page that does not describe the failure path is describing the demo.

## What neither should answer

A person following a script and an agent following an answer set have the same limit: they should not give regulated advice. Health, legal and financial questions get handed to someone qualified, and the script says so. For clinics and practices, whose calls are about treatments and whose records are health data, an off-the-shelf agent is the wrong tool; that is a bespoke build in an account the practice owns, and the receptionist page says the same.

## Is either one a marketing-call problem?

No. The rules that restrict automated telephony in the UK are written around calls an organisation makes: PECR regulation 19 covers recorded marketing messages sent by automated calling systems, and regulation 21 covers unsolicited marketing calls and the Telephone Preference Service screen. A caller who rang you has not been marketed at, so neither regulation reaches the inbound conversation, human or agent. The line is crossed the moment either one dials out to sell something, and an agent on our plan never does.

## A decision table

| Your calls are mostly…                                     | What fits                                                                |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Bookings and questions with fixed answers, at all hours    | An agent on a divert, with a person as the fallback                      |
| Long, emotional or a sales conversation in their own right | A person, whether an answering service or someone you employ             |
| A mix, with most of the misses out of hours                | An agent out of hours and on overflow, your team in hours                |
| Questions that need qualified advice                       | Neither; a person who is qualified, with the script saying so            |
| Patients, clients or tenants whose details are sensitive   | A build in your own account with your own answer set, not a hosted agent |

## Seven questions to ask either one

1. Can I hear the opening line, or read the script, before I buy?
2. What happens when the caller is upset, or asks something outside the script?
3. What is the unit of billing, and what sits outside it?
4. Are calls recorded, and how is the caller told?
5. Where does the message or summary go, and how quickly?
6. What happens to my number: divert, port, or keep?
7. If I cancel, what leaves with me?

An answering service and an agent will answer those seven differently, and the differences are the comparison.

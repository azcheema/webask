Until 22 September 2026 the rules for a veterinary practice website were the general ones. Since then they have had Article numbers, and several of the Articles come with dates. This post is the Order, quoted, with the dates in a table, and the one obligation that is not on a web page at all.

<Callout variant="note" title="TL;DR">
  The Veterinary Services Market Investigation Order 2026 was made on 22 September 2026 and came
  into force the next day. A practice with fewer than 15 sites has until 22 March 2027 to publish
  its price list one click from the homepage, its practice information and its ownership details,
  and until 22 September 2027 for the prescription notices. Groups with 15 or more sites have until
  22 December 2026 for the price list, practice information and care plan page; their ownership and
  complaints dates are the same 22 March 2027, and their prescription notices fall due on 22 June
  2027. The price list must sit on a "prices" or "fees" page and carry 36 named items.
</Callout>

## The Order is made, and the dates are in it

The Competition and Markets Authority made the Order on 22 September 2026, following its final report of 24 March 2026, and Article 1(2) says it "shall come into force on the day after the Order is made". It draws one line through the sector: a "Large Veterinary Business" has "15 or more FOPs and/or OOH Centres" — first-opinion practices and out-of-hours centres — and a "Small Veterinary Business" has fewer. Five rows of the Article 3 table reach a website, a booking system or the paperwork around a visit:

| Obligation                                                                                   | Large (15 or more sites) | Small (fewer than 15) |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | --------------------- |
| Ownership information online, on signage and at the premises                                 | 22 March 2027            | 22 March 2027         |
| Practice information, the price list, the parasiticide price list, pet care plan information | 22 December 2026         | 22 March 2027         |
| In-house complaint process and complaint logs                                                | 22 March 2027            | 22 March 2027         |
| Written estimates for higher-cost treatments and itemised bills                              | 22 June 2027             | 22 September 2027     |
| Written-prescription awareness, provision and the standard notices                           | 22 June 2027             | 22 September 2027     |

The CMA has said an updated explainer for practices is coming, and the British Veterinary Association publishes guidance on getting ready. Read the Order itself alongside them; a summary can drop an Article, and a website cannot.

## Where things go: two clicks, and one

Article 4(2) sets the rule for everything the Order puts online. It "must be: (a) easily accessible on or via the homepage, with any links to the relevant page labelled in a Clear and Prominent way and, unless stated otherwise in the Article, requiring no more than two clicks from the homepage; and (b) displayed in such a way that its content is Clear and Prominent." A practice inside a group meets that on its own website or, if it has none, on "the dedicated webpage for that FOP" on the group's site.

The price list is the exception that is stricter. Under Article 7(4) it "must be: (a) accessible in a maximum of one click away from the homepage of the website; and (b) hosted on a webpage which uses the terms 'price', 'prices', 'pricing' or 'fees' in page navigation information and page metadata". One click, on a page whose navigation label and metadata say what it is.

## The 36 items and their extras

Schedule 1 lists 36 services, products and procedures in five categories — consultation and preventative care, prescription, dispensing and administration, surgeries and treatments, diagnostics and laboratory tests, and end-of-life care — and several carry required extras: the standard appointment duration in minutes, a hyperlink to what a care plan includes, how asterisked items are priced, and the third party's details where a service is outsourced. Article 7(5) requires the list to be updated "before any revised price is charged", and Article 7(7) requires the website to say where a scheduled service is referred to a third party as a matter of course. On top of the Order sits a rule that applies to every trading website: a price shown online must say whether it includes VAT.

The CMA's release of 24 March 2026 recorded the starting point: "Less than 40% of practices have prices on their websites." The Order turns that into a dated obligation.

## Ownership everywhere Google looks

Article 5 does not stop at a footer line. Ownership Information must be "displayed in a Clear and Prominent manner" on the website of the group and of each practice, and the owning entity's name or the group's trading name must "(a) appear on the homepages … in a Clear and Prominent manner, including the top website banner where available; (b) be included within the metadata of all webpages on each website …, including metadata used by general search services and others; (c) appear in both paid-for and organic search engine results in a Clear and Prominent manner; and (d) appear in online map results". Article 5(5) excuses (c) and (d) where the practice cannot directly control the results, "provided that they have made all reasonable representations to, and requests of, the person who does control those matters". For a website that is the banner, every page's metadata and the structured data that feeds search and maps — a template change, not a paragraph.

## The obligation in your booking system

Article 7(3)(c) is the one a website audit will not catch, because it is not on a page. "When sending the first of any digital communications to a Pet Owner to confirm a consultation booking", the practice must "include in that communication: (i) the price the Veterinary Business will charge for the consultation; and (ii) a link to the webpage where the Price List is published." That is a template in the practice-management or CRM system that has to pull the right price and the right link, for every booking, from the first message. Check it in the booking system, not the browser.

## Care plans and savings claims

Article 9 defines a "Quantified Savings Claim" — an amount, a percentage or the size of the saving — and a "General Savings Claim", which says a saving exists without saying how much, and it attaches requirements to each for statements "on any website it operates". Whatever the claim, the "Pet Care Plan Standard Information" must be published "on the main page that provides visitors to the website with information about Pet Care Plan(s)". A plan page therefore carries the standard information as its main content, and any saving it claims is written to the definition it falls under, with the arithmetic kept where the practice can show it.

## The prescription literature and the complaints page

Under Article 14 the "RCVS Written Prescription Literature" must be displayed "in a Clear and Prominent manner" on each practice website "within a maximum of two (2) clicks from the homepage", updated within a month of the RCVS notifying a change, and the Order prescribes the "Standard Electronic Message" — "Written prescriptions are available. See the RCVS website for further information." — for the digital communications it specifies, plus a notification in the footer of every invoice or receipt for medication. Under Article 21 the practice needs a written in-house complaints process that tells owners "that Pet Owners have a right to complain and how they can complain", with a ten-working-day informal stage and an eight-week resolution window. For a small practice the prescription obligations fall due on 22 September 2027 and the complaints process on 22 March 2027.

## The RCVS rules that did not change

The Order sits on the Royal College's Code and its supporting guidance on advertising: "All advertising should be accurate, truthful, and not of a character likely to bring the profession into disrepute"; comparative claims "should have regard to section 3 of the CAP Code"; "specialist" or "advanced practitioner" in a practice name only with "genuine and meaningful involvement, and oversight, in case management by at least one RCVS specialist"; "Veterinary Hospital" only for practices accredited at that level; endorsements "underpinned by sound scientific principles or … a recognised evidence base". A rebuild for the Order is the moment to check every instance of those words.

## A checklist by date

**By 22 March 2027 (small practices; 22 December 2026 for large groups on the starred items):**

1. Ownership information clear and prominent on every site; the group or trading name in the homepage banner and in every page's metadata; representations made to the search and map providers.
2. \* A price list one click from the homepage, on a "prices" or "fees" page, with the 36 Schedule 1 items, their extras and the VAT position.
3. \* Practice information published and kept current.
4. \* The parasiticide price list with the VMD register link.
5. \* The pet care plan page carrying the Standard Information, with savings claims written to Article 9.
6. A written complaints process, online, with the stages the Order sets out.
7. \* The booking confirmation template carrying the consultation price and the price-list link.

**By 22 September 2027 (small practices; 22 June 2027 for large groups):**

8. The RCVS prescription literature within two clicks, and the standard electronic message and invoice footer in the templates.

The pages we read selling missed-call text-back to tradespeople are good at describing the problem. You are under a boiler, the phone rings out, the caller books the next name on the list. What they leave out is the rest of the sequence — the quote a reply becomes, the paperwork a quote carries, and the number on the van that the text has to come from. Here is the whole sequence, with the rules in.

<Callout variant="note" title="TL;DR">
  Divert the number you already publish rather than buying a new one. Send a text that says you
  missed the call and how to reply or book, with no offer in it, so it stays a service message. When
  the reply becomes a quote accepted by text or on the doorstep, the customer has a 14-day right to
  cancel, must be told so before they are bound, and must expressly ask you before you start inside
  those 14 days — on paper or by email where the quote was accepted on the doorstep. And the Gas
  Safe brand or your registration number appears only with your registered trading name, only while
  you are registered, in the form the scheme's guidelines set.
</Callout>

## What a missed call costs a trade

Not a percentage from a vendor page. Count your own for a week: the calls that rang out, the ones you rang back, the ones that had already booked someone else by the time you did. Put a rough value on the jobs among them. That number, which nobody else can supply, is the only honest case for the text-back — and it is the number the monthly report keeps counting afterwards.

## The divert, not a new number

For a trade the number is the one on the van, and a divert leaves it untouched: calls that ring out are forwarded to the platform, which sends the text from a number registered to you, and nothing painted on anything has to change — or change back if you stop. A new UK number is possible, registered to your business on the platform's regulatory record (a business not registered in the UK gets a UK mobile or freephone number instead), but for a trade it is a second line to register and pay for when the one on the van already works. The [text-back rules post](/blog/missed-call-text-back-uk-rules) covers the registration record and the message arithmetic in full.

## The text, word for word

> Hi, it's {Business}. Sorry we missed your call. Reply here and we'll get back to you, or book at {short link}. Reply STOP to opt out.

Everything in it is service content — who, why, reply, book, stop — and nothing in it is an offer, which is what keeps it a service message rather than an unsolicited marketing text. For a trade the temptation is the seasonal line — "boiler service £20 off this month" — and it is the one thing the template must never gain. The character arithmetic and the ICO's content test are in the text-back rules post; for the van, the point is that the text says your name and comes from your number.

## "Missed call text back for plumbers" — what the products sell and leave out

Of the four UK missed-call text-back products we checked in September 2026, one is sold to plumbers by name, one shows a roofer in its sample text, and none mentions PECR on its page. Two show a sample message carrying a "book today" line or an emoji. The product is the text; the rules are what make it safe to run at scale, unattended, on every call you miss.

## The reply that becomes a quote

Here is the part that follows the reply, and the one a text-back cannot do for you. The caller replies, you price the job, they say yes. If they said yes at their home, that is an off-premises contract; if they said yes by text, phone, email or through your website, and taking jobs that way is how you work, it is a distance contract. Under the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 either kind gives them the right to cancel "at any time in the cancellation period without giving any reason", and for a service that period "ends at the end of 14 days after the day on which the contract is entered into".

Three things follow. They must be given the information in Schedule 2 to the Regulations, with the cancellation right and a cancellation form, before they are bound, and it must be confirmed on paper or another durable medium such as email. If it is not, the period runs to "12 months after the day on which it would have ended", and a customer who cancels in that time pays nothing for the work done. And you must not start the work inside the 14 days unless they have "made an express request", on paper or by email for a doorstep quote — with that request in hand, a customer who cancels part-way pays "in proportion to what has been supplied". The exception is precise: a visit they "specifically requested" for "urgent repairs or maintenance" carries no right to cancel, but "services in addition to the urgent repairs or maintenance requested" do. And a doorstep job where the customer pays "not more than £42" sits outside both the information duties and the right to cancel; a quote accepted by phone, text, email or your website has no such floor.

None of that is exotic. It is a line in the quote message, a tick box that records the request to start, and a confirmation the system sends by itself — which is why the text-back and the quote flow should be built together.

One more thing the reply does. The ICO says of the soft opt-in that "A person doesn't need to actually buy anything from you. It's enough if 'negotiations for the sale' took place", and its examples include "requesting a quote". So a caller who asks for a quote, and who was given a simple way to refuse when their details were taken, can later be marketed to about similar services — but a missed call on its own is not a quote request, and the text-back must not assume it is.

## The van, the website and the register

Gas Safe Register's brand policy says: "Only registered gas businesses are permitted to display the appropriate Gas Safe Register brand and/or a Gas Safe registration number and these can only be used in association with the registered trading name." Its definition of marketing material includes "websites, adverts, vehicle signage, business stationery, social media, online website directories", and misuse by a registered business is treated as a breach of the Rules of Registration. TrustMark permits its logo only while a business is registered and reports misuse to Trading Standards. A competent person scheme lets a registered installer self-certify that work of the kinds the scheme covers complies with the Building Regulations, and the customer receives "a certificate within 8 weeks of completion".

On the van and on the site that means the brand or the number only alongside your registered trading name, laid out as the scheme's Brand Guidelines require, checked against the register on the day the page goes live, and never the words approved or accredited unless the scheme's own register uses them.

## What your website says becomes the contract

The Consumer Rights Act 2015 treats "anything that is said or written to the consumer, by or on behalf of the trader, about the trader or the service" as a term of the contract if the customer takes it into account. A promise on the site — same-day callout, a ten-year guarantee, no call-out charge — binds you as if it were in the quote. Write the site the way you write the quote.

## A checklist

1. The published number is diverted; no new number unless you want a second line.
2. The text names the business, gives a way to reply, book and stop, and carries no offer.
3. It fits one 160-character segment in the basic character set.
4. The caller's number is used to reply to that call and for nothing else unless they ask.
5. The quote message carries the cancellation information and records the request to start.
6. Urgent-repair visits are flagged as the exception; extra work on them is not.
7. The Gas Safe brand and number appear only with the registered trading name, in the scheme's form; any scheme's mark only while you are registered.
8. Nothing on the site promises what you would not put in the quote.

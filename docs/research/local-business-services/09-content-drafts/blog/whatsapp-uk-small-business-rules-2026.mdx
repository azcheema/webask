WhatsApp is a messaging channel with two sets of rules stacked on top of it: Meta's, which are contractual, and PECR's, which are law. It is also the channel whose price is quoted in two currencies depending on who you ask. This post sets out the three kinds of message, the opt-in, the two price cards side by side, and what changed on 1 October 2026.

<Callout variant="note" title="TL;DR">
  You may message someone on WhatsApp only after they gave you the number and opted in to messages
  from you, and PECR's rules on marketing apply on top. Meta prices delivered template messages per
  message by category; free-form replies inside a conversation, free since November 2024, are
  charged since 1 October 2026. Meta's UK card is in pounds; a platform that resells the channel
  bills in dollars with a mark-up. Never convert one into the other at a rate you picked.
</Callout>

## Three kinds of WhatsApp message

Meta's platform charges "on a per-message basis for each message we deliver to users", and, until 1 October 2026, only for template messages — the pre-approved formats a business sends to start a conversation — in three paid categories: marketing, utility and authentication. A reminder or an order update is a utility message; an offer is a marketing message; a one-time code is authentication. Each has its own rate per country.

The fourth kind is the reply. When a customer messages you, a 24-hour customer-service window opens, and what you send inside it is a "service" message in Meta's vocabulary — free-form, no template. "Service conversations are now free for all businesses" has been Meta's line since November 2024. There is also a "free entry point": when a customer starts the conversation from an advert that clicks to WhatsApp or a Facebook Page button and you reply within 24 hours, a 72-hour window opens in which every message is free.

That is the map. The 1 October change redraws one region of it, below.

## Opt-in first: Meta's rule, then PECR's

Meta's Business Messaging Policy is blunt: "You may only contact people on WhatsApp if: (a) they have given you their mobile phone number or username; and (b) you have received opt-in permission from the recipient confirming that they wish to receive subsequent messages or calls from you." It then hands the legal question back to you: "You are solely responsible for determining the method of opt-in, that you have obtained opt-in in a manner that complies with laws applicable to your communications." And it requires you to "respect all requests (either on or off WhatsApp) by a person to block, discontinue, or otherwise opt out of communications from you via WhatsApp".

In the UK the law in question is PECR. The ICO reads "electronic mail" to cover SMS and direct messages sent through social media, and its wording is technology-neutral, so the design here treats a WhatsApp message the same way: a marketing message to an individual — a sole trader, a partnership, anyone on a personal number — needs their prior consent to the UK GDPR standard, or the narrow soft opt-in for existing customers; a limited company can be marketed to without prior consent under PECR, but Meta's opt-in still applies to it. So the opt-in is written once, to both standards: what messages, from whom, on WhatsApp, with a way to stop — and it is recorded against the contact so that every send can check it. A tick-box that says "WhatsApp updates" may pass Meta's wording and does not pass PECR's; a box that names the business, the kinds of message and the channel, unticked until the customer ticks it, satisfies both.

## What changes on 1 October 2026

Meta's page on non-template messages puts it in one sentence: "Effective October 1, 2026, Meta will charge for service messages, which have not been charged since November 2024." The charging is "on a per-message basis for service messages, consistent with how Meta charges for template messages", and "By market, rates for service messages are the same as the rates for utility and authentication messages." The free entry point survives: messages inside the 72-hour window that an advert or a Page button opened stay free.

In plain terms: the replies you send inside a conversation, which had cost nothing for nearly two years, have cost a utility message each since 1 October. The platform we use says the same from its side — free-form replies "at no charge" "Through 30 September 2026", billable after the first 1,000 service messages per phone number each month, per its guide as modified 22 September 2026. For a business whose WhatsApp traffic is mostly replies to customers, the October change is the one that moves the bill; for one sending campaigns, the marketing rate already did.

## Utility or marketing: content decides both prices

Meta's categories and PECR's test point the same way. A reminder that says when the appointment is and how to change it is a utility message to Meta and a service message to the ICO. Add "and this month's offer" and it is a marketing message to Meta, priced higher, and direct marketing to the ICO, needing consent. The cheapest WhatsApp message and the lawful one are the same message, which is a rare alignment worth building around.

## The number

A WhatsApp Business number is a phone number registered to your business on the platform; on the one we use, a new UK number provisioned by the platform sits on a regulatory record with the business name, registration and a UK address, and a business without a UK registration uses a UK mobile or freephone number. Unlike a voice line, a WhatsApp number cannot sit behind a divert — the account is the number — so the decision is which number customers will message, and the honest answer is the one they already have.

## When WhatsApp is the wrong channel

For first contact. A missed-call text goes by SMS because it answers a call and reaches every mobile without an app or an opt-in; a WhatsApp message to a number with no opt-in breaks Meta's policy before it reaches PECR. For a campaign to a list that opted in to email and never to WhatsApp. And for anything the customer did not ask for, in any category, because the reply "who is this?" costs you a message and the relationship.

## A checklist before you switch it on

1. Every WhatsApp contact gave you the number and opted in to WhatsApp messages from you, in words that name the business and the kinds of message.
2. The opt-in is recorded on the contact and checked before every send.
3. Marketing messages go only to people you may market to under PECR; reminders and updates carry no offer.
4. You know which card you are paying from, in which currency, and at what mark-up.
5. You have read the 1 October 2026 change and budgeted for the replies.
6. Opt-outs are honoured on and off WhatsApp, and the contact is removed from the list.
7. The number customers message is the one they already know.

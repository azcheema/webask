"How to check Google Maps ranking" has a practical answer and an honest one, and the honest one has to come first: there is no single number. Google's own help page says local results are ranked by relevance, distance and prominence, and distance means that the list a customer sees depends on where the customer is standing. A position you check from your own desk is one position among many. This post is about checking it in a way that does not fool you, reading the number Google actually gives you, and understanding why nobody can promise you a spot.

## Why there isn't one ranking

Google describes the three factors plainly. "Relevance is how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for." "Distance refers to how far each business is from the customer who's searching." "Prominence means how well-known a business is." The middle one is the one that undoes the idea of a single rank: a plumber in the north of a town is closer to the customers in the north, and the map re-orders itself for every searcher. Two customers a mile apart can see two different top threes for the same words, and both are correct.

That is not a flaw in the checking; it is what the product is. So the useful question is not "where do I rank" but "where do I appear, for which searches, from which places, and is that changing".

## How to check it without fooling yourself

1. **Sign out, or use a private window.** Your own account's history nudges your results towards businesses you have looked at before, which includes your own.
2. **Search from where a customer would.** In Google Maps, move the map to an area you serve, so that the search runs from there, and type the words a customer would type: the service and the place, not your business name. Searching your own name tells you nothing about the ranking; it tells you the profile exists.
3. **Repeat from three to five areas.** The areas you actually serve, not the one you sit in. Note where you appear in the list and in the map's pins.
4. **Use the same five searches every time.** The services you sell, in the words customers use. Changing the words changes the result, and then nothing is comparable.
5. **Log it, weekly, in a table.** Search, area, position, date. Four columns. A month of that tells you more than any single check.
6. **Tell the local pack from the Maps list.** The three results Google shows on the main search page are a different list from the full Maps list behind them; note which one you are looking at.

Tools that scan a grid of points around your premises and report a position at each one exist, and some are useful for the trend. Their numbers are estimates from simulated locations rather than what a customer saw, so treat a grid as a weather map, not a measurement, and never as a promise.

## The number that matters more than the rank

Inside the profile, Google reports how people found you: how many searches showed your profile and which words they used, how many people viewed it, and how many called, asked for directions, clicked to the website or booked. Those are Google's own counts of real customers doing real things, and they move month to month for reasons you can act on. A rank that rises while calls fall is a rank that was checked from the wrong place. When we report on a profile, those are the fields we report, because they are the ones the customer can verify in their own account.

## What Google says moves it, and what you control

Google's page lists what it lists, and nothing on it is secret. Complete information, because a profile that says what you do, when you are open and where you are is easier to match to a search. The right categories, because relevance is matched on them. Photos, hours that are accurate, and posts that are current. And reviews: in Google's words, "more reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking", and "when you reply to customer reviews, it shows that you value their feedback". The way to get reviews is to ask everyone, incentivise nobody and hide nothing; the way to answer them is briefly, for the next reader.

What you do not control is distance. You cannot move your premises closer to every customer, and a service-area business that hides its address, as Google's guidelines require where customers are not served at the premises, competes on the area it declares. The guidelines also decide whether a profile should exist at all: a business with no premises customers visit and no area it travels to is not eligible, which is why we have no profile of our own and manage yours in your account.

## Why nobody can guarantee a position

Google's own sentence is the one to keep: "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google." A guarantee of a top-three position is a promise about a list that is different for every searcher and decided by a company that does not sell places on it. The person making the promise does not control the thing promised. That is not a criticism of ambition; it is the reason our profile page says what we manage and what we report, and does not say where you will rank.

## What a "Google Maps ranking service" can honestly sell

Management of the profile: checking it is eligible, completing it properly, keeping categories, services, hours and photos right, posting weekly, answering questions, running a review programme that asks everyone, and keeping your name, address and phone number consistent across the directories that copy them. Then reporting Google's own counts every month. That is work that moves the things Google says matter. It is not a rank, and the page that sells it should not call it one.

## A checklist for a monthly check

- The same five searches, from the same three to five areas, logged in the same table.
- The profile's own performance counts for the month: searches, views, calls, directions, website clicks, bookings.
- Every field on the profile still accurate, including holiday hours.
- New reviews answered; the request flow still asking everyone.
- Name, address and phone number still identical on the site and the main directories.
- One thing changed this month, so that next month's numbers mean something.

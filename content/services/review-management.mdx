## Good work, few reviews, and the bad one shows first

You finish a job well and nobody says so where it counts. The one customer who was unhappy wrote a review. Now that review is the first thing a stranger reads about you, and the customers who were pleased never wrote a word; nobody asked them to. Google review management fixes the asking: every customer is asked once for a review, and every Google and Facebook review gets a reply drafted for you to approve.

## Sound familiar?

**The electrician with twelve reviews.** A competitor across town has two hundred. You have never asked anyone for one.

**The dental practice that is nervous.** The team has heard that asking for reviews is risky and that offering anything is forbidden, so it does neither, and the rating drifts on a handful of old comments.

**The garage with the one-star rant.** It is from someone who never came in. It is still there, unanswered, and nobody is sure what to say or whether it can come down.

## How it works

1. **Every customer is asked, once.** A text or email goes out automatically after the job or the visit, timed for your service, with one link to the review form and nothing else.
2. **Nobody is filtered.** Happy or not, the same request at the same point. No private "rate us first" step, no second message to the ones who did not click.
3. **Every Google and Facebook review gets a drafted reply for you to approve.** Reviews from both sites arrive in one inbox. We draft a short reply in your voice within two working days; nothing is posted in your name until you say so.
4. **You see the counts.** Once a month, a report shows requests sent, reviews received, any change in the rating and the reviews still unanswered.

## What keeps you safe

The rules now name two shortcuts. Asking only the customers you know were happy is cherry-picking under the CMA's guidance and against Google's policy. Where a business offers a discount or a prize for a review, the CMA's guidance says the review must be clearly identifiable as incentivised — usually by a prominent label, as an advert. Google's policy says not to offer anything at all. The programme does neither, and we will say no if you ask us to add either. Genuine negative reviews stay up and get a reply; a review that breaks Google's own rules can be reported through Google's process, and whether it comes down is Google's decision.

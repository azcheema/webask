## Good work, few reviews, and the bad one shows first

You finish a job well and nobody says so where it counts. The customer who was unhappy, once, wrote three paragraphs. Now that review is the first thing a stranger reads about you, and the forty people who were pleased never wrote a word — because nobody asked them.

## Sound familiar?

**The electrician with twelve reviews.** A competitor across town has two hundred. The work is no better; they ask, every time, and you do not.

**The dental practice that is nervous.** The team has heard that asking for reviews is risky and that offering anything is forbidden, so it does neither, and the rating drifts on a handful of old comments.

**The garage with the one-star rant.** It is from someone who never came in. It sits at the top, unanswered, because nobody knows what to say or whether it can come down.

## How it works

1. **Every customer is asked, once.** A text or email goes out after the job or the visit — an hour later for a trade, a day later for a salon, whatever fits — with one link to the review form and nothing else.
2. **Nobody is filtered.** Happy or not, the same request at the same point. No private "rate us first" step, no second message to the ones who did not click.
3. **Every review gets a reply you have approved.** Google and Facebook land in one place. We draft a short reply in your voice within two working days; nothing is posted in your name until you say so.
4. **You see the movement.** Once a month: requests sent, reviews received, rating movement, reviews still unanswered.

## What keeps you safe

Two shortcuts every review tool makes easy are the two the rules now name. Asking only the customers you know were happy is cherry-picking under the CMA's guidance and against Google's policy. Offering a discount or a prize for a review has to be labelled as an incentive, and Google's policy says not to offer anything at all. The programme does neither, and we will say no if you ask us to add either. Genuine negative reviews stay up and get a reply; a review that breaks Google's own rules is reported through Google's process, and whether it comes down is Google's decision.

## Which platform, and what will the shop really cost to run?

Settle both before anyone designs a product page. A shop has to take a visitor from a listing to a confirmed order, with its reviews, prices, basket emails and tracking set up to follow UK rules. The starting price buys a Shopify build, handed over in your name; a headless front end (a storefront built as its own code, with Shopify behind it) or a fully custom build is an add-on. Beyond the build, a shop carries a platform subscription, the apps you run and payment fees, all in your own accounts.

## Sound familiar?

Three situations a small business selling online may recognise.

**The brand that has outgrown its theme.** The product pages are still the theme's defaults. The delivery charge first appears on the payment screen, which is the last point at which a customer learns what the order really costs. The reviews widget came with the theme, and nobody has checked what it shows.

**The WooCommerce shop nobody owns.** It was built years ago on WordPress. Plugins have piled up, updates are waiting, and nobody can say whether the advertising pixel loads before or after the cookie banner. It still takes orders, and any change has to keep it taking them.

**The seller moving off Etsy or Wix.** Orders come in, but the shop lives on someone else's platform. You want a shop of your own, keeping the catalogue and the brand.

## How it works

1. **We make the platform recommendation on the first call.** Where a themed Shopify store fits, we will say so, even when a bigger build would bill more. We recommend headless or a fully custom build only when the brand experience or the integrations genuinely outgrow a themed store.
2. **We design the templates that take money.** Listing, product, basket and checkout come first, not only the home page. Delivery costs show as a figure, or an honest estimate, as early as the shop can calculate one.
3. **We wire payments, delivery and tax.** Payment gateway, delivery rules and carrier rates, and tax calculation, each tested against real orders before launch. The tax setting and the price on the page are set up together, so consumer prices display VAT-inclusive.
4. **We build reviews in from the start.** Your review section launches empty, with an honest "no reviews yet", and each review is stored with proof of purchase. The star ratings that search engines read appear only once real reviews exist.
5. **You launch, and we stay for thirty days.** Through the first weeks of live trading we cover bug fixes, small tweaks and team training. The store, domain, analytics and customer data sit in accounts you own from day one.

## Shopify or WooCommerce: which suits a UK small business?

Shopify is the starting recommendation, because it runs the payments, fraud tooling and platform patching for you; WooCommerce suits a business already on WordPress or with unusual product logic, but then everything is yours to look after. It runs on WordPress, which we do not build in, so where WordPress is a requirement a WordPress specialist is the honest option. Where Shopify fits, moving an existing WooCommerce shop across is an add-on we scope with you.

| Platform                     | Choose it when                                                                | What you look after                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Shopify, custom-themed**   | You want payments, fraud tooling and patching to stop being your problem      | Less: Shopify patches the platform, but reviews, consent and tax display follow the apps you add, so each app needs checking before it goes in |
| **WooCommerce on WordPress** | You already run WordPress, have unusual product logic, or want to own the lot | All of it — consent, reviews, VAT display, updates — so someone has to own it                                                                  |
| **Headless**                 | The storefront experience is the product, or speed is a competitive edge      | The storefront code itself; tracking runs only where that code is written to allow it                                                          |

## What keeps you safe

- **Reviews.** Since 6 April 2025 UK law has banned fake reviews and hiding that a review was incentivised, and the CMA can fine without going to court. We will not seed or write reviews, or carry over any not traceable to a real customer, and will decline a build that depends on them.
- **Consent and email.** "Reject all" is as prominent as "Accept all", and we test that nothing non-essential, such as tracking or advertising code, runs before a visitor agrees. Every email sign-up is captured with its opt-out at the point of collection and stored with the basis it was collected on.
- **Accessibility.** In UK terms the accessibility floor is the Equality Act 2010, so the checkout gets a manual keyboard pass against WCAG 2.2 AA.
- **Your terms.** Your returns policy, terms and consumer-rights wording need someone qualified to sign them off.

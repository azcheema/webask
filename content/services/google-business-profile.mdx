## Half-filled, unloved, and below a competitor on the map

Someone claimed the profile years ago. The hours are wrong since the summer, the photos are the ones from opening day, three questions from customers sit unanswered, and the business two streets away — no better at the work — shows above you every time someone searches for what you both do.

## Sound familiar?

**The unclaimed profile.** Google made one from directory data. The category is wrong, the phone number is the old one, and nobody in the business has the login.

**The second branch.** The first profile took years to settle. The new one has two photos, no services listed, and reviews meant for it landing on the other.

**The suspended profile.** It disappeared from the map one Tuesday with an email nobody understood, and the appeal form has been open in a tab for a month.

## How it works

1. **We check you qualify, first.** Google's test is short: a premises customers can visit, or a business that travels to them. We tell you on the first call whether your address passes, before anything is created.
2. **Your account, our access.** You hold the owner role; we are added as a manager, which is how Google expects a third party to work with the owner's consent. You can remove us in a minute without losing anything.
3. **The full first pass.** Categories chosen against what people search, every service listed and described, attributes, opening and holiday hours, photographs, a description written for a customer — and a name, address and phone that match your website character for character.
4. **Then every week.** A post about something real, new photographs from a short brief to you, every customer question answered before a stranger answers it, and suspicious competitor listings reported through Google's process.
5. **You see what Google sees.** Once a month, from Google's own insights: how people found the profile, what they searched, how many called, asked for directions or clicked through.

## What the starting price buys

The eligibility check, the audit and full first pass in your account, the weekly post and questions answered, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yell and FreeIndex kept consistent by hand, and the monthly report. Per location, because the work is per profile.

**Add when you need it:** a suspension worked through and appealed · listings synced by tool across a long directory list · extra locations. Each is a line on the plan.

## What keeps you safe

A profile for an address nobody works from — a virtual office, a mailbox, your accountant's address — breaks Google's rules and can be suspended, and a suspension runs on Google's timetable, not ours. So we never create one, never verify a location we have not confirmed you operate from, and never hold the owner role on a profile that belongs to you. We have no profile of our own for exactly the same reason: WebAsk works remotely with no UK office, so Google's test rules us out, and we would rather say so than pretend.

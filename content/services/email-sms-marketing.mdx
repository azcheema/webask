## Hundreds of past customers, and nobody messages them

They are all in the system: the people who booked last year, the ones who asked for a quote and went quiet, the customers who would come back if someone reminded them. The CRM is live and silent. Writing to them is the job that never gets to the top of the list, and when it does, nobody is sure who is allowed to be written to.

## Sound familiar?

**The dentist's lapsed patients.** Two hundred people overdue a check-up. The recall system reminds them once; nothing follows it up.

**The salon's quiet month.** February is empty every year and everyone knows it in January. The list of regulars could fill it, if anyone wrote to them.

**The trade's annual reminders.** Every boiler serviced last October is due this October. The dates are in the system; the reminders are not.

## How it works

1. **Month one is the audit, not a send.** Every record on your list is tagged by who it belongs to — a company, or a person — and by whether you may message them. Records with no basis are parked, not deleted, and not messaged to ask.
2. **Two campaigns a month, written for you.** You send a few lines on what the month is about; we write each campaign in your voice, check it against the rules for your sector, send it from your platform to the people who may receive it, and report two weeks later.
3. **One automation a month, built or reviewed.** A welcome after an enquiry, a follow-up after a quote, a rebooking prompt at the interval your service runs on, a win-back when a customer goes quiet — each with the who-may-receive-it check in front of it.
4. **A list kept clean.** Bounces removed, opt-outs honoured in every tool, and every message naming who sent it and how to stop.
5. **A report that counts what you can check.** Replies, bookings and enquiries that followed a campaign, opt-outs, and the size of the list you may still message — not open rates, which inboxes now block or pre-fetch.

## What keeps you safe

Who you may message depends on who they are, not what the address looks like. A limited company can be emailed. A sole trader, a partnership or anyone on a personal address or number needs to have consented, or to have bought from you and been told they could refuse at the time and in every message since. A bought list never qualifies, and asking someone for permission by email is itself a marketing email. The audit sorts this out before anything is sent, and every automation checks it before it fires. For a clinic, a message that promotes a treatment is an advert, and the compliant message promotes the consultation. [Who you can lawfully email or text in the UK →](/services/crm-automation)

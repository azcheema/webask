## Start with what UK search actually looks like

Google holds roughly **92.7% of UK search** (Q1 2025, Searchlab / DigitalApplied) — more dominant than in the United States. **AI Overviews now appear on 15–25% of queries**, with some April 2026 measures putting it as high as 48% (SQ Magazine).

Google's own AI search guidance, published on 15 May 2026, is direct about what that means: the same ranking systems power AI Overviews and AI Mode, and the generative features retrieve from the Search index. Index well and you are already optimising for the AI surfaces. So there is no "AEO" or "GEO" package bolted onto the retainer here, and none of the three things currently marketed as AI optimisation: no `llms.txt` — Google has said it does not use it — no AI-specific schema, no chunked question-and-answer rewrites.

What the UK picture does change is Bing. ChatGPT and Copilot both run on the Bing index, so Bing Webmaster Tools is a first-week job — its own property, its own sitemap, its own coverage alerts. Google's AI surfaces still have the highest UK reach by a distance; the next two sit behind a search engine that rarely gets a second thought.

## Local search without a Google Business Profile — starting with ours

Most businesses with premises qualify for a Google Business Profile. If that is you, it is the single most valuable thing you can fix in local search, it costs nothing, and it should be finished before you spend a pound on a retainer — categories and service areas set properly, a name, phone and address matching your website character for character, and location pages for it to rank alongside.

One rule there we will not bend: reviews are never bought and never incentivised. Since 6 April 2025 the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 has made fake and undisclosed incentivised reviews illegal, with the CMA able to fine directly — up to 10% of global annual turnover or £300,000, whichever is higher. An agency offering to seed your reviews is offering to create a liability.

**We have no profile of our own, and we are not going to get one.** WebAsk is operated remotely: no UK office, no staffed address. Google requires a real location with staff present during stated business hours, and virtual offices, mailbox services, unstaffed coworking and PO boxes are policy violations that risk permanent suspension. There is no eligible profile for us to create.

The cost is real, and we would rather state it than bury it: no Map Pack eligibility and no Maps pin, so the three-result block above the blue links is unreachable on any query; no Google reviews, the strongest local trust signal an SMB has when choosing a supplier; no knowledge panel.

So local search here is an organic-only problem: location pages with genuine local substance, topical depth, and listings where the eligibility rules are actually met. That is a constraint we work under, not a preference — the organic-only route is the only route open to us, in the same UK SERPs we would be working in for you.

What makes it survivable is buyer behaviour: someone choosing an agency researches for weeks rather than tapping a pin the way someone searching for an emergency plumber does. If you sell same-day services from a shopfront the profile is the whole game — and we will tell you that rather than sell a retainer around it.

## British English is a ranking signal, not a style preference

Google uses spelling as one of its language-identification signals, and a page written consistently in UK English reads as `en-GB`.

The part that costs money is the keyword research: **`optimisation` and `optimization` are not the same keyword and do not carry the same volumes.** Pull your numbers on the US spelling and you build a content plan for the wrong country. Every pull has to run with a UK locale filter, and the variant carrying UK volume is the one that belongs in the title and the H1 — not the one that read more familiar to whoever wrote the brief. The same applies to head terms: UK searchers skew towards "web design" where the US market skews towards "web development", for identical intent.

Consistency is the part that quietly slips: the body copy is British, and the theme boilerplate, meta descriptions, JSON-LD and transactional emails are whatever the plugin shipped with. Mixed variants dilute the signal, and auditing it takes an afternoon.

One structural point: a `.co.uk` is the strongest UK geo-targeting signal available, applied automatically and impossible to override — the International Targeting setting was removed from Search Console in 2022. Sell only to the UK from a `.com` and your language signals carry weight the domain would otherwise carry for free.

## What your analytics can and cannot tell you under PECR

UK cookie rules are stricter than the US equivalent, and they change what honest reporting looks like. Nothing non-essential may be set before the visitor chooses, and declining has to be as easy as accepting — the ICO's principal enforcement focus under PECR.

The consequence is an SEO reporting problem more than a legal one: on a compliant site a share of your visitors is never measured at all, so organic sessions are a sample rather than a census, and the sample moves with your consent rate. An agency quoting exact conversion counts off a consent-gated analytics install is presenting a subset as the whole. Two readings survive that — the trend against a stable consent rate, and Search Console, which counts impressions and clicks before a banner is ever rendered. Both belong in a report, and the gap between them should not be modelled away.

## Replacing a site without losing what it already ranks for

A site due for SEO work is often also overdue for replacement, and the two jobs collide. Done carelessly, a migration loses positions that took years to earn; done properly, replacing the platform is usually the largest Core Web Vitals gain on the table. The rules are unglamorous, and they are the whole difference:

- **Crawl the old site before anything is switched off.** A WordPress sitemap under-reports what is indexed: author and date archives, tag pages, feeds, attachment pages and paginated variants are crawled without ever appearing in it. The crawl export, not the sitemap, is the redirect map.
- **Every legacy URL gets a permanent redirect, held for at least twelve months.** 301 or 308, never 302 or 307. Keeping them longer costs nothing.
- **Pages that were never real content get a 410, not a 404.** A 404 tells Google to keep checking back for months; 410 says gone, and deindexes fastest.
- **Do not block the old paths in `robots.txt`.** It is the instinctive tidy-up and it backfires: block the crawl and Googlebot never sees the 410, so the URLs stay indexed indefinitely.

Expect rankings to move for **two to four weeks** after cutover and then settle, usually higher, on the improved Core Web Vitals. Search Console's Change of Address tool does not apply when the domain stays the same and only the platform changes — there is nothing to file, and the redirects carry it.

## The one vertical where keyword strategy is a compliance exercise

If you run an aesthetic clinic, the keyword map is partly a legal decision. The CAP Code does not permit prescription-only medicines to be advertised to the public, so some of the highest-volume terms in that market are ones no UK clinic can legally target — and the ASA treats the familiar euphemisms as implied promotion, closing the obvious workarounds too. It has upheld complaints on wording as indirect as a single adjective.

The rule is not dormant: the MHRA issued 47 enforcement notices to aesthetic businesses in 2024, mostly on exactly this, and the ASA now runs proactive AI monitoring that finds non-compliant advertising without waiting for a complaint. What remains targetable is consultation-led, and it maps onto pages that rank normally. CAP also runs a free pre-publication Copy Advice service, worth using before a title tag goes near production.

## You need one page, fast, and your website cannot give you one

There is an offer to run, an event to fill or an advert to switch on, and the page it needs does not exist. The website was built two years ago by someone who no longer answers, adding a page means a ticket and a wait, and the campaign is next week.

## Sound familiar?

**The seasonal offer.** Boiler services before winter, a spring treatment, a Black Friday price. It needs its own page with one button, and the home page has eleven.

**The open day.** A date, a map, a form, and a reminder text to the people who booked. Nothing on the site does any of that.

**The Google Ads campaign with nowhere to land.** The advert is written and the budget is set; the click goes to a home page that talks about everything except the thing in the advert.

## How it works

1. **One page, one job.** No navigation to wander off into. A headline that matches the advert or the offer, the reasons, the form or the booking calendar, and one ask.
2. **Built the right way for the job.** In the platform's page builder inside your account when the traffic comes from an advert, an email or a QR code and getting live this week is the point; in code alongside a site we built when the page has to rank in search or live inside a site whose standards you paid for. The first call decides, and the reason goes into the scope.
3. **Wired to your CRM.** The form or the calendar feeds the pipeline directly, with consent captured at entry in words the person can understand and a follow-up message that says who sent it and how to stop. Nothing is retyped.
4. **Measured.** Every button and submission is an event, the campaign tags are handled, and thirty days after launch you get a report: visits, submissions, bookings, sources, and what we would change.

## What the starting price buys

One page in the builder, the form or calendar wired to your CRM with consent at entry, the thank-you and follow-up message, tracking and campaign tags, a report at thirty days, and two rounds of revisions on copy you send us.

**Add when you need it:** a coded page alongside a site we built · a three-step funnel (landing, offer, thank-you) · copy written from a brief rather than edited from your draft · a payment or deposit step. Each is priced as what it is.

## What keeps you safe

A campaign page measures itself, and measurement sets cookies. Anything non-essential waits for a clear yes, with accept and reject given equal weight and nothing pre-ticked; since February 2026 a plain visit count can run without consent if the visitor is told and can object, but an advert platform's conversion pixel still waits. If the page takes a deposit or a booking from a consumer who is not in front of you, the cancellation right and the terms go on the page, not behind a link. We read the builder's default settings before launch rather than trusting them. [Landing page or website — which do you need? →](/blog/landing-page-or-website-small-business)

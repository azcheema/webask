Private dentistry is under active regulatory examination right now, and your website is the
main artefact a regulator, a competitor or a complainant would look at first.

That is not a scare tactic. It is a description of what is currently happening.

## The CMA is examining your pricing, until March 2027

In **March 2026** the Competition and Markets Authority opened a market study into the
**£8.4bn** private dentistry sector. It is due to report by **March 2027**. Pricing
transparency is explicitly in scope, alongside whether patients can make informed choices and
whether providers engage in misleading or unfair conduct.

The CMA has already published the numbers driving its concern: the price of an initial
consultation rose **more than 23% between 2022 and 2024**, and routine check-ups by **more than
14%**.

Now consider where a patient actually encounters your pricing. It is your website. A site that
shows no prices, or shows a single "from" figure that bears no relationship to what anyone
pays, is not a neutral design choice during a market study into pricing transparency — it is a
worked example of the thing being studied.

The practices that come out of this well will be the ones whose websites already answer the
question clearly. That is a straightforward build decision, and it is one you can make now
rather than in 2027.

## Four regulators, one website

Aesthetic clinics deal principally with one problem, applied strictly. Dentistry deals with
four bodies at once, and they overlap on the same pages.

| Body    | What it reaches on your site                                                                  |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **GDC** | Claim language, genuine and non-incentivised reviews, use of "specialist", consent for images |
| **CQC** | Registration in England is a legal requirement — and displaying it is a strong trust signal   |
| **ASA** | All promotional material legal, decent, honest and truthful                                   |
| **CMA** | Pricing transparency and consumer protection — and, since April 2025, fake reviews            |

Plus UK GDPR for patient data and PECR for recall and marketing messages. Most practice
websites we audit are fine on two or three of these and quietly wrong on the rest.

## Three things we find on almost every practice site

**"Specialist", used where it is not supported.** Describing a registrant as a specialist — or
implying it with phrasing like "our specialists in implants" — when they are not on the GDC
specialist list for that field is a standards breach. It is also trivially checkable by anyone
who cares to look, including a complainant. We audit every instance of the word during a build.

**Incentivised review campaigns.** This one now binds twice. The GDC requires reviews to be
genuine and not incentivised, so a prize draw or a discount in exchange for a review is a
standards problem on its own. Separately, since **April 2025** the DMCC Act 2024 makes
commissioning or publishing fake reviews illegal, with the CMA able to determine a breach and
fine directly — up to **10% of global turnover or £300,000**. A review flow that asks every
patient at the right moment, with nothing attached, satisfies both and works better anyway.

**Before-and-after images without marketing consent.** Consent to treatment is not consent to
publish. The images also need to be representative: showing an exceptional outcome in a way
that implies a typical one is where practices get into difficulty, under both GDC standards and
the ASA.

## What we build

- **The website.** Fast, mobile-first, on Next.js — with layout shift, blocking time and page
  weight enforced as build-failing budgets, and load time held to a two-second target that is
  settled in the field rather than on a build server. New patients arrive on a phone, frequently
  in pain, and rarely in a patient frame of mind.
- **Pricing pages that answer the CMA question.** Clear treatment pricing, presented so a
  patient can genuinely compare and understand what they will pay.
- **Credentials, displayed properly.** Named clinicians with GDC numbers and CQC registration
  shown as trust architecture rather than buried in a footer. This is also a real search signal:
  verifiable credentials attached to named people are exactly what quality evaluation looks for.
- **Recall and reminder automation.** Wired into your practice management system, so recall,
  reminders, reactivation and post-treatment follow-up run without the front desk chasing.
  Cutting failed appointments and reactivating dormant patients usually moves the number further
  than new-patient advertising, because you have already paid to acquire those people.
- **Local SEO.** Technical foundations, treatment and area pages mapped to real UK search
  demand, and a compliant review flow feeding it.

Search _dental practice Altrincham_ and Google answers twice: a block of three businesses on a map, then a column of ordinary blue links underneath. The two are assembled by different systems — the map draws on Google Business Profiles and on how far the searcher sits from a pin, the links below on websites. Effort that lifts one does not automatically lift the other, so the checklist here is sorted by which it feeds.

Google's grip on UK search is heavier than its grip on the American market, so this is a checklist about Google, with Bing flagged where it earns a mention.

One disclosure before step one. WebAsk holds no Google Business Profile and will not be applying for one — fully remote, no staffed UK address, so there is no listing we are eligible to create. The [SEO service page](/services/seo) sets out what that costs. If your business has premises, the profile is the largest single lever in local search, and nothing below argues otherwise.

## 1. The profile — if you qualify for one

Google's rule is narrow and worth reading before you claim anything: the listing must correspond to a place where somebody actually works during the hours you publish. A mailbox service, an unstaffed hot desk or a PO box will not do, and being caught risks permanent suspension of the listing. If you travel to customers instead, the service-area setting hides the address rather than inventing one.

After that the work is unglamorous: the narrowest primary category that fits plus honest secondaries, hours that match reality including bank holidays, services listed individually, real photographs rather than stock, and the Q&A block pre-answered with the questions your phone already fields.

## 2. Reviews — in the UK this is a legal step, not a marketing one

If you have read local SEO advice published in the US, you will have met the suggestion to offer a small discount in exchange for a review. Do not import it. Since **6 April 2025** the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 has made it unlawful to write, commission or publish fake consumer reviews, or to conceal the fact that a review was incentivised — and an incentive includes cash, a discount, a freebie, an event invitation or any financial interest. Presenting reviews misleadingly is caught as well, aggregate star scores included.

What makes this sharper than a platform policy is the enforcement route: the CMA can determine a breach and impose a penalty without going near a court, and in March 2026 it opened investigations into five named businesses over review handling.

Regulated sectors carry a second layer. The GDC treats genuine, non-incentivised reviews as a matter of professional conduct, so [dental practices](/industries/dental-practices) answer to the regulator and the CMA at once. What stays entirely legal is the part the rules were never aimed at: ask every patient or customer, ask promptly, keep it to a single tap, and reply to all of them.

## 3. NAP, the British version

Name, address, phone — consistent everywhere. Three details decide whether a UK NAP stays that way.

- **Name.** Settle on your registered name or your trading name, then use that one everywhere. Appending _best dentist Manchester_ to a profile name breaches Google's naming rules and invites a suspension.
- **Address.** Royal Mail format, postcode spaced properly (`M1 2AB`, never `M12AB`). The county line has not been needed for delivery in years, and adding it to some listings but not others is the easiest way to end up with two versions of one address.
- **Phone.** One number, written one way. Call-tracking numbers scattered across directories break that consistency by design; if you need tracking, use dynamic insertion that leaves the canonical number in the markup. International form drops the leading zero after `+44`, and the American 555 convention does not exist here.

<Callout variant="warning">
  Publishing an address because the law requires it is not the same as being local to it. The
  Electronic Commerce (EC Directive) Regulations 2002 require the service provider's name,
  geographic address and email to be easily, directly and permanently accessible — a legal
  disclosure that confers no local ranking benefit whatsoever. Renting a virtual office to claim a
  city fails on both counts at once.
</Callout>

## 4. Citations a British searcher actually recognises

An international directory does nothing for you in the UK. The list that counts starts with **Yell** — the most widely recognised name, and one that accepts service-area listings — then **FreeIndex**, then the long tail of **Thomson Local**, **192.com**, Scoot and Cylex. Sector registers outrank all of them for trust: CQC registration for dental practices in England (in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland the equivalents are Healthcare Improvement Scotland, Healthcare Inspectorate Wales and the RQIA), JCCP and Save Face for aesthetics, plus LinkedIn for entity reinforcement.

<Callout variant="tip">
  Bing Places deserves twenty minutes despite Bing's traffic share, because ChatGPT and Copilot both
  run on the Bing index. Apple Business Connect feeds Siri and Apple Maps, and Foursquare is
  reported to supply 60–70% of ChatGPT's local recommendations. Check each one's presence rules
  before submitting — the test that rules out a profile rules these out too.
</Callout>

With no eligible profile and no Google reviews to gather, the professional-directory tier — Clutch, DesignRush, G2 — sits at the top of WebAsk's own citation order rather than at the bottom. Reorder yours the same way if the map is closed to you.

## 5. Area pages that earn their place

One contact page cannot rank for six towns; a page per area can, provided it says something specific — the districts you cover, the sectors that cluster there, the services you deliver locally. Slug conventions matter too: `/locations/manchester`, not the American `city-state` pattern, which reads as an import to the audience you are addressing. London is the exception, worked borough by borough rather than as one page, because head terms there are the most expensive in the country. The same rule is applied here: WebAsk publishes [an area page](/locations) only where there is something specific to say about the area it names, which is why there are three of them rather than thirty.

## 6. Structured data — and the property to omit when you have no premises

If you have a real address, mark it up so nothing is inferred:

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "LocalBusiness",
  "name": "Example Dental Practice",
  "address": {
    "@type": "PostalAddress",
    "streetAddress": "1 Example Street",
    "addressLocality": "Manchester",
    "addressRegion": "Greater Manchester",
    "postalCode": "M1 2AB",
    "addressCountry": "GB"
  },
  "areaServed": "Greater Manchester",
  "telephone": "+44-161-496-0123"
}
```

That block belongs to a business with premises. Without them, delete the `address` object rather than filling it with something plausible and emit `areaServed` alone — which is what this site does: WebAsk's organisation markup declares `areaServed` as the United Kingdom, with no `address` and no `geo` anywhere.

## 7. The technical floor

None of the above survives a slow site. This one is built to LCP ≤ 2.0s, INP ≤ 200ms and CLS ≤ 0.05. Layout shift, main-thread blocking and script weight fail the build on every pull request; LCP is tracked there as a warning, because a localhost run under mobile throttling is not the number that decides anything; and INP can only be measured from real sessions, so no lab test asserts it at all. A budget checked once at launch and never again is not a budget. If your site is an ageing WordPress install, the platform is part of the weight, which is why WebAsk is replacing its own 2020-era WordPress build at webask.co.uk.

## What to leave out in 2026

- **Bulk citation packages.** Hundreds of automated listings on directories no British customer visits will not move you, and inconsistent copies of your NAP actively hurt.
- **Anything that buys or rewards a review without saying so.** Since April 2025 an undisclosed incentive is not a guideline breach; it is an enforcement risk with a fine attached — and for GDC registrants, incentivised reviews are a conduct problem however they are labelled.
- **Keyword-stuffed profile names.** Fast to do, fast to get suspended for.
- **Chasing every algorithm rumour.** The fundamentals above move slowly. Section 2 is the exception, and it moved because the law changed — not because an algorithm did.

## Where to start this month

Three moves, in order. Complete the profile if you are eligible. Fix your NAP everywhere it appears, starting with Yell and your own footer. Then build one honest page per area you serve, adding the next only once the first has something real to say.

A mobile mechanic works from a van. A cleaner works from home and in customers' houses. An online shop runs from a laptop. None of them has a shopfront, and all three want to show up when someone nearby searches for what they do.

So how do you rank in the Map Pack in the UK without a shopfront? The first question is a plainer one: will Google give you a Business Profile at all? This post answers both, in Google's own words wherever it can.

Checked against Google's published guidelines on 1 October 2026. These guidelines are Google's own rules for its platform, not the law, and Google can change them.

<Callout variant="note" title="TL;DR">
  If you travel to your customers, Google allows one service-area profile and says you should hide
  your address; if you work online only, Google says you are not eligible, and a virtual office or a
  remote mailbox is not an eligible address. Without a profile, in WebAsk's reading, the local
  results have nothing of yours to rank. Either way, Google says nobody can pay for a better local
  ranking, and the website is the part you own.
</Callout>

## Google's test: customers in person, at your place or theirs

Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en-GB) (read 1 October 2026) open with the test: "If your business either has a physical location that customers can visit, or travels to customers where they are, you can create a Business Profile on Google."

Its [eligibility guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en-GB) (read 1 October 2026) put it another way: "To qualify for a Business Profile, a business must make in-person contact with customers during its stated hours."

So the test is meeting customers in person, at your place or at theirs. The mechanic and the cleaner pass, because they travel to their customers. The online shop does not.

## What Google says about local ranking

"Map Pack" is the industry's name for the block of local businesses that Google shows with a map. Google's own phrase is "local results on Google Maps and Search", on its page of [tips to improve your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en-GB) (read 1 October 2026). That page explains three factors under the headings Relevance, Distance and Prominence:

- "Relevance is how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for."
- "Distance refers to how far each business is from the customer who’s searching."
- "Prominence means how well-known a business is."

The same page says: "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google." In WebAsk's reading, these local results rank Business Profiles, so a business that cannot have one has nothing there to rank. And qualifying for a profile does not promise you a place in them. Google does say what helps: "Businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results."

The ordinary links under the map work differently, as the [local SEO checklist](/blog/local-seo-checklist-2026) explains.

### Two cases Google's pages leave open

**A service with a minimum age.** Google's guidelines say: "Businesses associated with products or services that require the customer to be a certain minimum age, like alcohol, cannabis or weapons, aren’t permitted as service-area businesses without a shop front." Its pages, as read on 1 October 2026, give only those three examples. They do not say which other services the line covers.

**Customers at your home, with no sign.** Google says businesses showing their address "should maintain permanent fixed signage". Its [service areas page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en-GB) (read 1 October 2026) says of a hybrid business: "If your business doesn’t have permanent on-site signage, it's not eligible as a shop front and should be listed as a Service Area Business." But it defines a service-area business as one that "visits or delivers to customers directly but doesn’t serve customers at their business address". A home studio that never travels fits neither the signage line nor the service-area definition as written.

If either case is yours, ask Google first, through the "Contact us" link at the foot of its guidelines page.

## If you travel to customers: the service-area profile

Google's guidelines say: "If you’re a service-area business, you should hide your business address from customers."

Hidden does not mean made up. Google asks you to "Create your Business Profile for your actual, real-world location." For a plumber who works from home, that location is the home.

The service areas page sets out how the areas work:

- "You can set up to 20 service areas based on the cities, postcodes or other areas that you serve."
- "You can’t set your service area as a radius distance around your business."
- "The boundaries of your overall area shouldn’t be more than about two hours of driving time from where your business is based." The guidelines add: "For some businesses, larger service areas may be appropriate."
- "When you set your service area, be as specific and accurate as possible."

What does a service area do? In Google's words, "it helps people find your Business Profile." Google does not say that it makes you rank in each place you list. Google measures distance between the business and the person searching. But none of the pages read for this post says what point it uses for a business that hides its address. This post does not guess.

Hide your address and enter no service area, and "a local area is chosen for you". That is from Google's page on [managing your business address](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2853879?hl=en-GB) (read 1 October 2026).

One base gets one profile. Google allows more only "If you have different locations for your service business, with separate service areas and separate staff at each location". And it says: "Do not create more than one page for each location of your business, either in a single account or multiple accounts."

### Verifying a business with no shopfront

Next, Google asks you to verify the business. Its [verification page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242?hl=en-GB) (read 1 October 2026) says: "Verification methods are automatically determined by Google and can’t be changed." Where video is open to you, Google recommends it.

The [video verification page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14271705?hl=en-GB) (read 1 October 2026) says it works for "Service Area Businesses" as well as shopfronts. The video must be filmed live, with no edits, "At least 30 seconds long", and recorded and uploaded from a mobile device through your profile. For a service-area business, it must show:

- where you operate, such as "street signs, nearby landmarks or other identifiers at your business address";
- your "professional tools, equipment or products";
- that you manage the business, such as "unlocking a van that has your business branding", or a document "that matches the name on your Business Profile".

Working from home? Google's tip: "If you run a Service Area Business from your home, you can record landmarks and signs in your immediate neighbourhood to verify the address." An online-only business, the same page says, is "not eligible for a Business Profile and can’t be verified."

Verification reviews "take up to five working days", Google says, and "On rare occasions, reviews may take longer."

## Why a rented or borrowed address is not a shortcut

If you work online or from home, a city-centre address can look like a way onto the map. Google's [policies overview](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13762416?hl=en-GB) (read 1 October 2026) says what happens to a PO Box address, and to a business that is not where it claims to be:

- "Merchants that set their business address at a PO Box will have their profile suspended."
- "If we determine that your business doesn't exist in the location that you claim, Google will disable your profile."

A friend's address is not your "actual, real-world location" either. The two lines above are what Google says it will do.

Adding keywords to your business name is another shortcut. Section 3 of the local SEO checklist covers Google's rule on names.

## Not eligible? Where you can still be found

WebAsk sits in the online-only row. We work fully remotely, with no UK office to visit, and we do not travel to customers. So we fail the in-person test, and we have no Google Business Profile. We will not create one. Our organisation markup names the United Kingdom as the area we serve, with no address. Like any business that cannot have a profile, we compete for local searches with pages.

Below the local results sit the ordinary results, and those rank web pages. That is the part of search a business with no profile can work on:

- a page for each service you offer;
- a page for each area you genuinely serve, with something specific to say about it;
- your name, phone number and website address, written the same way everywhere.

WebAsk has [three area pages](/locations), each written because there was something specific to say about that place. If your site cannot carry pages like these, that is a job for [web development](/services/web-development). None of this guarantees a position. The [local SEO checklist](/blog/local-seo-checklist-2026) covers area pages in section 5 and the rules on reviews in section 2.

### Structured data with no address to mark up

Google's [LocalBusiness documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business) (read 1 October 2026) lists two required properties: `address` and `name`. It says: "You must include the required properties for your content to be eligible for display as a rich result." `areaServed` is not listed there, or in Google's [Organization documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization) (read 1 October 2026).

So, as section 6 of the checklist says, with no premises you leave `address` out rather than invent one. That has a cost: without `address`, the markup is not eligible for Google's local business rich result. `areaServed` still says honestly where you work, but neither page says Google uses it. And Google's eligibility test turns on meeting customers in person, not on markup.

The Organization page has "no required properties" and recommends "an indication of real-world presence (for example, address or telephone)". With no premises, give your phone number.
